\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why economic pricing matters.}
A scalar leaderboard rewards accuracy at any cost. Production buyers
care about whether an agent is \emph{worth deploying}: how often does
it commit and succeed? How costly are its mistakes? What is the
spread of its expected value? PROPHET makes these questions central
by paying the agent for being honest about its odds. Calibration
becomes \emph{the business metric} rather than a side ablation.

\paragraph{Why we don't publish a single scalar.}
We deliberately publish a Pareto frontier on
$(\mathrm{net\_payoff}, \ECE)$ rather than a single ranking. This
matches the production reality that the right answer depends on the
deployment cost-of-error: a safety-critical system needs both low ECE
and high net-payoff (Pareto-corner); a research-bot can tolerate
higher ECE if its net-payoff covers the cost of human review.

\paragraph{Why a Brier-based proper scoring rule.}
Brier is bounded, $C^\infty$ smooth, and admits closed-form expected
payoff at known $P[y=1\!\mid\!t]$. Logarithmic score is also proper
but unbounded at $\Phat \to \{0, 1\}$, which makes it gameable when
the agent reports near-extreme probabilities on tasks of borderline
difficulty. CRPS (continuous ranked probability score) is preferable
for continuous outcomes; we adopt it in a forthcoming v2 for the
writing family.

\paragraph{What this benchmark cannot answer.}
PROPHET does not measure: (i) long-horizon coherence across many days
of interaction (see CHRONOWEAVE in our companion design report);
(ii) drift detection under silent environmental change (see DRIFT-
WORLD); (iii) safety in catastrophic-risk scenarios (use targeted
red-team suites). PROPHET is one of three suites in the AGENCY-3
family; it covers calibrated commitment, period.
