\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why economic pricing matters.}
A scalar leaderboard rewards accuracy at any cost. Production buyers
care about whether an agent is \emph{worth deploying}: how often does
it commit and succeed? How costly are its mistakes? What is the
spread of its expected value? PROPHET makes these questions central
by paying the agent for being honest about its odds. Calibration
becomes \emph{the business metric} rather than a side ablation.

\paragraph{Why we don't publish a single scalar.}
We deliberately publish a Pareto frontier on
$(\mathrm{net\_payoff}, \ECE)$ rather than a single ranking. This
matches the production reality that the right answer depends on the
deployment cost-of-error: a safety-critical system needs both low ECE
and high net-payoff (Pareto-corner); a research-bot can tolerate
higher ECE if its net-payoff covers the cost of human review.

\paragraph{Why a Brier-based proper scoring rule.}
Brier is bounded, $C^\infty$ smooth, and admits closed-form expected
payoff at known $P[y=1\!\mid\!t]$. Logarithmic score is also proper
but unbounded at $\Phat \to \{0, 1\}$, which makes it gameable when
the agent reports near-extreme probabilities on tasks of borderline
difficulty. CRPS (continuous ranked probability score) is preferable
for continuous outcomes; we adopt it in a forthcoming v2 for the
writing family.

\paragraph{Why two difficulty regimes.}
The Standard tier of 12 families measures calibration at near-ceiling
accuracy (90--97\,\% for the frontier panel). This is the realistic
deployment regime for instruction-following workloads --- ECE
discrepancies of $7\times$ across systems still surface and matter,
because production cost is dominated by the rare-but-costly
miscommit. The T\textsubscript{extreme} tier, where frontier accuracy
drops to $\sim$15--45\,\% by construction, deliberately mimics the
research / hard-problem regime: tasks at the saturation frontier where
even the best 2026 systems must hedge or abstain. By publishing both
regimes we (a) protect the benchmark from one-shot saturation as
models improve, (b) document the calibration-accuracy correlation
\emph{break} at the hard tail, and (c) supply a reference difficulty
floor for future calibration work. The expected joint pattern is that
Pareto ordering changes between regimes: a model that is well-calibrated
on easy tasks may over-commit on extreme ones, and vice versa.

\paragraph{What the data tells us about reasoning mode.}
The Standard tier shows that reasoning-tuned variants
(\texttt{deepseek-r1}, \texttt{qwen3-thinking-2507},
\texttt{kimi-k2-thinking}) tend toward \emph{quote} mode with
high $\hat P$ rather than abstention; they buy accuracy at the cost
of an inflated commit rate. Whether this translates to better net
payoff is mediated by the calibration-payoff trade-off (see
Section~\ref{sec:results}). On the T\textsubscript{extreme} tier we
expect the lift to be smaller: when a problem is genuinely beyond
the model's grasp, extended chain-of-thought makes the answer
\emph{more confidently wrong} rather than right.

\paragraph{What the data tells us about open vs closed.}
On the Standard tier, the open-weight \texttt{deepseek-v3.2} ranks
4th by net payoff despite lower accuracy ($84.6\,\%$ vs.\ Gemini's
$97\,\%$) --- its ECE-corrected commits dominate the field at
low cost per question. This is the cost-per-quality story
production buyers want. We expect the gap to widen on
T\textsubscript{extreme}: open-weight models lacking RLHF-grade
calibration tend to maintain $\hat P \approx 0.9$ across the board,
which is a strict loss on harder tiers where actual accuracy is
$\sim$20\,\%.

\paragraph{What this benchmark cannot answer.}
PROPHET does not measure: (i) long-horizon coherence across many days
of interaction (see CHRONOWEAVE in our companion design report);
(ii) drift detection under silent environmental change (see DRIFT-
WORLD); (iii) safety in catastrophic-risk scenarios (use targeted
red-team suites). PROPHET is one of three suites in the AGENCY-3
family; it covers calibrated commitment, period.
