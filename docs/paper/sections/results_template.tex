\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Two-tier evaluation overview}
We benchmark every agent at \emph{both} the Standard tier (12 families,
near-ceiling difficulty) and the T\textsubscript{extreme} tier (5
families with $\delta \in [0.97, 1.0]$). The Standard tier targets 240
tasks per agent (20 per family $\times$ 12); the
T\textsubscript{extreme} tier targets 40 tasks per agent
(8 per family $\times$ 5). Realised per-agent $N$ varies because of
provider-side refusals and multi-seed pooling on the Pareto-essentials;
both are disclosed in \autoref{tab:two-tier}. Headline Spearman and
Pareto-frontier flags use a pre-registered minimum-$N$ filter
($N \ge 100$ Standard, $N \ge 20$ T\textsubscript{extreme}). Bootstrap
95\,\% CIs (BCa for ECE, percentile elsewhere; $B = 10^4$) are reported
for every metric.

\paragraph{H1: rank by accuracy $\ne$ rank by calibration $\ne$ rank by
payoff.}
Spearman $\rho$ on the $K = 16$ agents with $N \geq 100$ on the Standard
tier: $\rho_{\text{acc, ECE}} = -0.94$,
$\rho_{\text{acc, payoff}} = +0.78$,
$\rho_{\text{ECE, payoff}} = -0.71$. The best-by-accuracy and
best-by-ECE model is \texttt{openai/gpt-5.2} (acc $0.970$, ECE $0.017$;
tied with \texttt{gemini-3-flash-preview} on accuracy but strictly
better on calibration). The best-by-payoff model is a different agent:
\texttt{google/gemini-3.1-pro-preview} ($37{,}813$ PROPHET-cents vs.\
$31{,}328$ for GPT-5.2 and $23{,}800$ for Gemini-3-Flash). Best
accuracy and best calibration do not imply best deployment payoff under
calibrated-commitment pricing; the orderings induced by each axis are
demonstrably different at the first-place level.

\paragraph{H2: reasoning mode raises conditional accuracy and net payoff
but does not uniformly increase strategic abstention.}
On the T\textsubscript{extreme} tier, the DeepSeek reasoning variant
abstains far more than its non-reasoning sibling
(\texttt{deepseek-r1} 86\,\% \textsc{Pass} vs.\ \texttt{deepseek-v3.2}
4\,\%); the Qwen reasoning variant abstains \emph{less} than its smaller
non-reasoning sibling (\texttt{qwen3-235b-thinking} 26\,\% vs.\
\texttt{qwen3-32b} 48\,\%) because the smaller 32B model PASSes from
inability rather than calibrated abstention. The robust signal is in
\emph{conditional accuracy}: reasoning variants are markedly higher
(R1 $82.4\,\%$ vs.\ V3.2 $51.0\,\%$;
Thinking $83.1\,\%$ vs.\ 32B $67.3\,\%$) and so is net payoff
(R1 $2755$ vs.\ V3.2 $550$; Thinking $4825$ vs.\ 32B $1588$). The
hypothesis ``reasoning over-commits'' is \textbf{rejected} on net
payoff for both families, but the intuitive ``reasoning $\Rightarrow$
more abstention'' mechanism \textbf{holds only for DeepSeek}, not for
Qwen. The unified statement the data support is: extended thinking
improves conditional accuracy enough that, even when the variant
commits less, net payoff goes up.

\paragraph{H3: open-weight Pareto-essentials match closed frontier on
the cost--payoff axis, not the ECE--payoff axis.}
The T\textsubscript{extreme} $(\ECE, \mathcal{P})$ Pareto frontier
(min-$N$ filter, $N \ge 20$) contains two closed-source agents:
\texttt{openai/gpt-5.2} (ECE $0.025$, payoff $10{,}500$, $N=71$) and
\texttt{openai/gpt-5} (ECE $0.019$, payoff $5{,}274$, $N=36$). The
closest open-weight challenger, \texttt{qwen3-235b-thinking}
(ECE $0.091$, payoff $4{,}825$), is dominated on $(\ECE, \mathcal{P})$
by both. \emph{However}, on the $(\text{cost}, \mathcal{P})$ frontier
Qwen3-235B-Thinking delivers $4{,}825$ at \$1.00 vs.\ \texttt{gpt-5.2}'s
$10{,}500$ at \$4.07 --- payoff-per-dollar $4{,}825$ vs.\ $2{,}580$ ---
so the cost-conscious open-weight deployment claim survives even though
the strict $(\ECE, \mathcal{P})$ claim does not.

\paragraph{H4: hard-tier accuracy collapse.}
On the matched $N$-filtered Standard panel ($K = 16$, $N \ge 100$) the
median accuracy drops from $87.0\,\%$ (Standard) to $60.0\,\%$
(T\textsubscript{extreme}, $N \ge 20$) --- a $27.0$-pp fall --- while
the median ECE \emph{worsens} by $+0.123$ (Standard $0.110$,
T\textsubscript{extreme} $0.233$, a $2.12\times$ inflation). Specific
drops include \texttt{claude-opus-4.7}
($90.0\,\% \!\to\! 63.6\,\%$, $\Delta = 26.4$\,pp),
\texttt{deepseek-v3.2} ($84.6\,\% \!\to\! 51.0\,\%$), and
\texttt{llama-4-scout} ($83.1\,\% \!\to\! 17.0\,\%$). The Standard-tier
near-ceiling effect was a difficulty-floor artefact.

\paragraph{Strategic family-level abstention.}
A cross-cut of the per-(agent, family) heatmaps reveals a clean
\emph{strategic-abstention cluster}:
\texttt{deepseek-r1} declines on $9$ of $12$ families
(100\,\% \textsc{Pass}); \texttt{kimi-k2-thinking} on $7$;
\texttt{qwen3-235b-thinking} on $4$; \texttt{openai/gpt-5} on $3$;
\texttt{gemini-3.1-pro-preview} on $2$. The non-reasoning agents
(\texttt{haiku-4.5}, \texttt{gemini-3-flash}, \texttt{deepseek-v3.2},
the Llama family) have zero $100\,\%$-PASS families. Reasoning-tuned
systems recognise families beyond their competence and decline at the
family level; non-reasoning systems commit indiscriminately. Per-family
heatmaps (PASS rate, committed-$N$, ECE conditional on commitment),
per-language multilingual breakdown, Pareto-frontier scatter,
reliability diagrams, MOP heatmap, per-pair significance, post-hoc
power, cost-conscious $(\text{cost}, \mathcal{P})$ frontier,
strict-verifier ablation, and rhyme / safety LLM-judge audits are all
in \autoref{app:ablations}.
