\section{Task families}\label{sec:families}

PROPHET ships twelve procedurally generated task families. Each family
exposes (a) a deterministic generator from a seed, (b) a
\emph{mechanical} verifier returning a Boolean, and (c) a
reference-panel-calibrated difficulty per task. The selection criterion
was the intersection of three constraints: capability coverage of the
production-LLM surface (code, math, knowledge, reasoning, writing,
tool-use, web, multimodal, data, scientific, multilingual, safety);
mechanical verifiability (regex, parser, integer-equality, or sandboxed
execution --- no LLM-judge in the primary loop); and lit-anchored
difficulty headroom~\cite{enigmata,zebralogic,nppc,ojbench,lcbpro,
aethercode,nolima,olym_math}. Excluded under these constraints
were multi-turn dialog, open-ended summarisation, RAG, and long-horizon
agentic planning (v2 candidates). \autoref{tab:families} gives the
family palette; generator and verifier source is in the public release.

\paragraph{Standard tier (T0--T7).}
Difficulty climbs from trivial (T0) to ``standard hardest'' (T7) within
each family. Examples: math runs from one-step arithmetic to
olympiad-lite sum-of-divisors; code runs from output-prediction on
small Python snippets to write-and-test; reasoning runs from sequence
puzzles to $3\times 3$ ARC-grid transforms and transitive deduction
with red herrings. Knowledge uses synonym-set verifiers; writing uses
regex / parser / acrostic / JSON-schema / ABAB-rhyme (the rhyme
verifier was upgraded post-pilot from suffix-match to a four-rule
nucleus-aware checker, Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). Tools and
browser run on in-memory simulators with action-replay verifiers; a
T7 browser task plants an ``IGNORE PRIOR INSTRUCTIONS'' injection that
is never the right answer.

\paragraph{T\textsubscript{extreme} tier ($\delta \in [0.97, 1.0]$).}
Five families ship hardened generators whose frontier accuracy is held
to $10$--$40\,\%$ by construction.
\emph{math}: Euler totient over a non-uniform range, multiplicative-order
$\mathrm{ord}_p(a)$ and modular quadratic residues over primes
$p \in [10^3, 5\!\cdot\!10^3]$, $12$-modulus CRT chains, fundamental
Pell solutions ($x^2 - Dy^2 = 1$), lattice-path counting with forbidden
cells on $n = 8$--$12$ grids, and discrete logarithms modulo primes
$p \in [5\!\cdot\!10^3, 2\!\cdot\!10^4]$.
\emph{code}: subset-sum count over $8$--$12$ items, modular knapsack
($11$--$15$ items, weights $\le 30$, modulus $5$--$11$), and DAG path
counting on $12$--$16$ nodes.
\emph{knowledge}: $6$-word letter-count chains
($c_1c_2 - c_3c_4 + c_5c_6$), obscure-fact compositions sourced from
$12$ lesser-known historical figures, and $3$-step word manipulations.
\emph{reasoning}: $5\times 5$ Latin-square cell-fill, depth-$9$ temporal
chains with $11+$ clues, $3 \times 3$-digit cryptarithmetic
multiplication, and $5\times 4$ Einstein/zebra puzzles with $8$--$12$
clues (uniqueness verified by backtracking during procgen).
\emph{multimodal}: $7\times 7$ grid transform chains (apply $9$
sequential transforms then read one cell) and $9\times 9$ Sudoku
cell-fill with $50$ masked cells, target provably unique.
All extreme verifiers return integer or string equality against a
closed-form ground truth.

\paragraph{Other families.}
\emph{tools}: pure-Python registry of $13$ fixed tools (arithmetic,
table lookup, filter, sort, dedup, count); three exhibit subtle
pathologies (off-by-one, semantic red-herring, false-success).
\emph{data}: procedurally generated CSVs ($3$--$30$ rows) embedded as
fenced \texttt{\`{}\`{}\`{}csv} blocks; tasks cover lookup, aggregation,
group-by, two-table join, and time-series outlier detection.
\emph{scientific}: physics / chemistry / biology / earth-science with
closed-form numerical answers ($\pm 10^{-3}$ relative tolerance).
\emph{multilingual}: a $200$-word EN / ES / FR / DE / HI / JP dictionary
supports language-id, multiple-choice translation, cognate selection,
cross-lingual arithmetic, ordering, and grammar. A v2 expansion to ten
languages (adds AR / ZH / RU / KO) is in Appendix~\ref{app:multi-v2}.
\emph{safety}: $50/50$ split of benign and refusal-required tasks; the
latter use synthesised placeholder phrasings
``\texttt{<SYNTHETIC\_PROHIBITED>}''. The mechanical verifier accepts
\texttt{PASS} or any of $14$ refusal substrings, with
$C_{\mathrm{failure}}=200$ so wrongly committing on a refusal task is
penalised twice as heavily.
