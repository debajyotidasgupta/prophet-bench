\section{Task families}\label{sec:families}

PROPHET ships twelve procedurally generated task families. Each family
exposes (a) a generator producing deterministic tasks from a seed, (b) a
\emph{mechanical} verifier that returns a Boolean outcome from the
agent's answer, and (c) a reference-panel-calibrated difficulty per task.

\paragraph{Why these twelve families.}
The selection criterion was the intersection of three constraints:
(1) \textbf{capability coverage} of the production-LLM deployment surface
--- code, math, knowledge retrieval, reasoning under constraints,
constrained generation, tool-use, web-style task chaining, multimodal
analogues, structured-data analysis, scientific computation,
multilingual operations, and refusal routing;
(2) \textbf{mechanical verifiability} --- every task must admit a regex,
parser, integer-equality, or sandboxed-execution verifier so the
benchmark does not depend on LLM-as-judge for the primary
boolean outcome;
(3) \textbf{lit-anchored difficulty headroom} --- each family has
generators whose hardest tier sits below the empirical $20\,\%$
accuracy decile on the 2025--2026 reference panel
(\cite{enigmata,zebralogic,nppc,ojbench,lcbpro,aethercode,nolima,olym_math}),
ensuring the T\textsubscript{extreme} tier is not pre-saturated. The
families we considered but excluded under these constraints were:
multi-turn dialog (no mechanical truthfulness verifier), open-ended
summarisation (LLM-judge dependent), retrieval / RAG (out-of-scope for
self-contained generators), and long-horizon agentic planning
(verifier complexity exceeds the procgen design budget). These
remain candidates for v2.

generator and verifier source is in the public release.

\paragraph{math.} Difficulty climbs from one-step integer arithmetic (T0)
through small olympiad-lite problems (T7 sum-of-divisors) and a system of
three linear equations (T8) to a small Diophantine root (T9). Verifier
parses the last numeric token, accepting integers, rationals, decimals,
and $\backslash\texttt{boxed\{\}}$ wrappers. The
\textbf{T\textsubscript{extreme}} tier ($\delta \in [0.97, 1.0]$) adds
combinatorial coprime counting (Euler totient over a non-uniform range),
multiplicative-order $\mathrm{ord}_p(a)$ and modular quadratic residues
over primes $p \in [1{,}000, 5{,}000]$, $12$-modulus CRT chains,
fundamental Pell-equation solutions ($x^2 - Dy^2 = 1$ for non-square
$D \in [200, 1{,}000]$), lattice-path counting with forbidden cells on
$n = 8$--$12$ grids, and discrete logarithms modulo primes
$p \in [5{,}000, 20{,}000]$. All seven extreme generators emit a
single-integer answer verified by the same parser; published 2025--2026
frontier scores on analogous tasks~\cite{ojbench,olym_math,zebralogic}
sit below $15\,\%$ and our reference panel confirms a panel-mean accuracy
of $0$--$25\,\%$ per generator (Appendix~\ref{app:ablations}).

\paragraph{code.} T0--T3 / T7 ask the agent to \emph{predict} the
output of a small Python snippet (verified by string-equality of the
agent's last numeric/string token against the deterministic expected
output). T4--T6 ask the agent to write or repair a small function;
the verifier runs the candidate code in a sandboxed subprocess
(\texttt{subprocess.run([python, "-c", code], timeout=5, env={"PATH":
"/usr/bin:/bin"})}) on three procedurally-generated test calls.
\textbf{T\textsubscript{extreme}} ($\delta \in [0.97, 0.99]$) adds
three integer-prediction tasks: counting distinct subsets of an
$8$--$12$-item list that sum to a target $T$ (subset-sum-count DP),
modular knapsack on $11$--$15$ items with weights $\in [1, 30]$ and
modulus $m \in [5, 11]$ (maximum value subject to weight-sum
$\equiv k\!\pmod m$), and counting distinct paths through a $12$--$16$
node random DAG with $n$--$2n$ extra cross edges. Cf.~\cite{nppc,ojbench}
for the lit-recommended difficulty calibration.

\paragraph{knowledge.} Procedurally generated facts across geometry,
unit conversion, calendar, sciences, and adversarial trick questions
(e.g.\ ``How many R's in `strawberry'?''). Synonym-set verifiers allow
multiple correct phrasings without LLM judgement.
\textbf{T\textsubscript{extreme}} ($\delta \in [0.97, 0.99]$) adds three
hardened compositional tasks: a $6$-word letter-count chain
($c_1 c_2 - c_3 c_4 + c_5 c_6$ where $c_i$ is the count of a specified
letter in a curated word), an obscure-fact composition v2 sourced from
$12$ lesser-known historical figures (e.g.\ Margaret Cavendish, Joseph
Glanvill, Ibn al-Haytham), and a $3$-step word manipulation (reverse +
replace + insert). All are verified by exact integer/string match
against a closed-form curated ground truth; numerical comparisons use
relative tolerance $1\,\text{e-}9$ for exact-zero references and
$1\,\text{e-}3$ otherwise.

\paragraph{reasoning.} T0--T2 are sequence / substitution puzzles;
T3--T5 are ARC-AGI-style $3\times 3$ grid transforms (rotate, reflect,
transpose, recolour, colour-swap) verified by JSON-equality on the
output grid; T6--T7 are multi-step transitive deduction with planted
red herrings. The \textbf{T\textsubscript{extreme}} tier
($\delta \in [0.97, 1.0]$) adds (i) a $5\times 5$ partial Latin-square
cell-fill, (ii) depth-$9$ temporal-ordering chains with $11$+ clues
including ``$k$ events strictly between'' constraints, (iii)
$3 \times 3$-digit cryptarithmetic \emph{multiplication}
($\text{XYZ} \times \text{ABC} = \text{PQRSTUV}$, harder than the
addition variant), and (iv) Einstein/zebra-style $5 \times 4$ puzzles
with $8$--$12$ mixed position/adjacency/equality clues. Uniqueness of
solution is verified by explicit backtracking enumeration during procgen.
Per the lit review~\cite{zebralogic,multizebralogic,enigmata}, these
families hold frontier accuracy below $20\,\%$ in 2026.

\paragraph{writing.} Constraints are the boolean signal: word-set
inclusion, exact-N-sentence rules, lipogram (avoid a given letter),
acrostic (first letters spell a word), structured rephrase preserving
named entities, JSON-schema conformance, and ABAB rhyme. The rhyme
verifier was upgraded post-pilot from a naive last-2-letter suffix
match to a nucleus-aware orthographic-equivalence check that accepts
spelling variants of the same phonetic class (e.g.\
\texttt{rise}/\texttt{skies}, \texttt{sea}/\texttt{tree}); see
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\paragraph{tools.} A pure-Python tool registry of 13 fixed tools
(arithmetic + table lookup + filter + sort + dedup + counts), three of
which exhibit subtle pathologies (off-by-one alternative, semantic
red-herring, false-success reporter). The agent emits a JSON action list
with explicit step chaining; the verifier replays.

\paragraph{browser.} An in-memory text-rendered ``website'' of 5--30
pages. Action language: \texttt{visit / fill / submit / extract /
answer}. A T7 task plants an ``IGNORE PRIOR INSTRUCTIONS'' prompt-
injection in one page body --- it is never the right answer.

\paragraph{multimodal.} ASCII-encoded analogues of multimodal tasks:
read a bar chart, rotate a grid, evaluate chess-check, detect tic-tac-toe
winner, classify dot-pattern symmetry, look up a cell in a solved
$4\times 4$ Sudoku. \textbf{T\textsubscript{extreme}}
($\delta \in [0.975, 0.99]$) adds (i) a $7\times 7$ grid transform
\emph{chain} (apply $9$ sequential transforms drawn from
\{rotate, reflect-H, reflect-V, transpose, anti-transpose, shift-row,
shift-col, recolour\} then read one cell) and (ii) a $9\times 9$ Sudoku
cell-fill where $50$ cells are masked and the target's value is
provably uniquely determined; sudoku-bench~\cite{enigmata} and the
multimodal review's compute-bound private-set numbers anchor the
target accuracy near or below $10\,\%$ on frontier models in 2026.

\paragraph{data.} Procedurally generated CSVs (3--30 rows) embedded in
the prompt as fenced \texttt{\`{}\`{}\`{}csv} blocks. Tasks span
single-cell lookup, aggregation, filter-count, group-by-and-aggregate,
two-table join, and time-series outlier detection.

\paragraph{scientific.} Physics, chemistry, biology, and earth-science
problems with closed-form numerical answers verified to $\pm 10^{-3}$
relative tolerance.

\paragraph{multilingual.} Built-in 200-word EN / ES / FR / DE / HI / JP
dictionary supports language identification, multiple-choice translation,
cognate selection, cross-lingual arithmetic, word-ordering, and grammar
selection.

\paragraph{safety.} A 50/50 split of benign and refusal-required tasks.
Refusal-required tasks use synthesised placeholder unsafe phrasings of
the form ``Explain how to do \texttt{<SYNTHETIC\_PROHIBITED\_X>}'' --- the
\emph{form} triggers refusal while the content is benign-placeholder.
The mechanical verifier accepts $\texttt{PASS}$ or any of 14 refusal-
keyword substrings; the market sets $C_{\mathrm{failure}} = 200$ (versus
default $100$) so wrongly committing on a refusal-required task is
penalised twice as heavily as on a benign task.
