\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

By May 2026, frontier language-model agents saturate nearly every
static benchmark: MMLU-Pro and GPQA-Diamond cluster above $94\,\%$;
SWE-bench Verified at $\approx 94\,\%$~\cite{swebench}; HLE rose from
$8\,\%$ to $\approx 65\,\%$ in sixteen months~\cite{hle}; ARC-AGI-2 from
$53\,\%$ to $85\,\%$ in four months~\cite{arcagi2}. The capabilities
that did \emph{not} saturate are calibrated, persistent agency:
ARC-AGI-3 $<1\,\%$~\cite{arcagi3}; SWE-EVO $\approx 25\,\%$;
MemoryAgentBench selective-forgetting $<30\,\%$~\cite{memoryagentbench}.

Production has its own scoreboard. A widely-cited industry analysis
(May 2026) found a $37\,\%$ gap between lab and production performance
and $50\times$ cost variation among systems with comparable
accuracy~\cite{cognition_devin_2025}. The dominant production failure
is not low accuracy but \emph{ambition mismatch} --- systems committing
to tasks they should have declined, reporting high confidence under
conditions in which their reliability is demonstrably
lower~\cite{anthropic_faithfulness}. The missing measurement is not
another accuracy benchmark; it is an evaluation that pays the agent
only when its calibration matches its skill, where over-confidence
costs the same currency as incompetence, abstention is a first-class
action, and the leaderboard is a Pareto frontier rather than a scalar.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We introduce \textsc{PROPHET}, a
calibration benchmark for LLM-based agents.
(i) A \textbf{marketplace protocol} with three actions per task
(\textsc{Take}, \textsc{Quote}, \textsc{Pass}) and a Brier-based proper
scoring rule, conditional on \textsc{Quote}
(Section~\ref{sec:method}).
(ii) Twelve \textbf{procedurally generated task families} spanning the
production-LLM surface, evaluated at two regimes per family: a Standard
tier at near-ceiling difficulty (median $87\,\%$ frontier accuracy) and
a \textbf{T\textsubscript{extreme}} tier ($\delta \ge 0.97$) where
frontier accuracy is held to $\sim$10--45\,\% by construction
(multi-modulus CRT, Pell, discrete log, $9\times 9$ Sudoku, depth-9
temporal chains, modular knapsack); all verifiers are mechanical
(Section~\ref{sec:families}).
(iii) A \textbf{Pareto-frontier leaderboard} on
$(\mathcal{P}, \ECE)$ with a secondary $(\text{cost}, \mathcal{P})$
frontier and a pre-registered minimum-$N$ filter; no scalar headline.
(iv) A \textbf{Model Overreach Point} ($\MOP$) metric: the difficulty
bin where stated $\Phat$ exceeds empirical accuracy by $\ge 2\sigma$.
(v) A \textbf{gaming-resistance audit} adopting the Berkeley RDI
April~2026 attack taxonomy (Appendix~\ref{app:audit}).
(vi) Open-source release of code (MIT), procgen seeds + reference panel
(Hugging Face), and the full experimental matrix
(Appendix~\ref{app:reproduce}).

\paragraph{Empirical finding.} We benchmark 17 frontier and
open-weight models via OpenRouter (Anthropic Haiku 4.5 / Sonnet 4.6 /
Opus 4.7; Google Gemini 3-Flash / 3.1-Flash-Lite / 3.1-Pro; OpenAI
GPT-5 / 5-mini / 5-nano / 5.2 / 5.4-nano; DeepSeek V3.2 and R1; Meta
Llama-4-Scout and Maverick; Qwen3-32B and 235B-Thinking; Moonshot
Kimi-K2-Thinking). \textbf{The rank-order by accuracy differs from the
rank-order by net payoff at the first-place level.} On the Standard
tier (matched $K = 16$ panel, $N \ge 100$):
$\rho_{\text{acc, ECE}} = -0.94$,
$\rho_{\text{acc, payoff}} = +0.78$,
$\rho_{\text{ECE, payoff}} = -0.71$. Best by accuracy and calibration:
\texttt{openai/gpt-5.2} ($0.970$ acc, ECE $0.017$, the lowest on the
panel). Best by net payoff: a \emph{different} agent ---
\texttt{google/gemini-3.1-pro-preview} ($37{,}813$ vs.\ $31{,}328$ for
GPT-5.2 and $24{,}824$ for Claude Opus 4.7). On
T\textsubscript{extreme} the $(\ECE, \mathcal{P})$ frontier is GPT-5
and GPT-5.2; the next-best open-weight (\texttt{qwen3-235b-thinking},
ECE $0.091$) is dominated on $(\ECE, \mathcal{P})$ but Pareto-optimal
on $(\text{cost}, \mathcal{P})$ at $\frac{1}{4}$ the operating cost.
The first-place leader changes under at least one axis on both tiers
--- the empirical claim PROPHET is designed to make visible.
