\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

In May 2026, frontier language-model agents have saturated nearly every
static benchmark we built for them.  MMLU-Pro and GPQA-Diamond cluster
above 94\,\%; SWE-bench Verified is at $\approx 94\,\%$ (state-of-the-art
2026 systems)~\cite{swebench}; Humanity's Last Exam rose from 8\,\% to
$\approx 65\,\%$ in sixteen months~\cite{hle}; ARC-AGI-2 went from
$53\,\%$ to $85\,\%$ in four months~\cite{arcagi2}.  The capabilities that did \emph{not} saturate are
all about calibrated, persistent agency: ARC-AGI-3 sits at $<1\,\%$
~\cite{arcagi3}; SWE-EVO at $\approx 25\,\%$; MemoryAgentBench's
selective-forgetting axis at $<30\,\%$~\cite{memoryagentbench}.

Production deployment has its own scoreboard. A widely-cited industry
analysis (May 2026) found a 37\,\% gap between lab benchmark scores and
production performance and a $50\times$ cost variation among systems
with comparable headline accuracy~\cite{cognition_devin_2025}. The
dominant production failure mode is not low accuracy but \emph{ambition
mismatch} --- systems that commit to tasks they should have declined,
that report high confidence under conditions in which their reliability
is demonstrably lower, that ``can't tell you what they don't know.''
Anthropic's faithfulness work~\cite{anthropic_faithfulness}
identified the mechanism: unfaithful chains-of-thought are
systematically \emph{longer} than faithful ones; models verbalise
hints that demonstrably change their answer in only 25--39\,\% of
cases. Verbose confidence is decoupled from competence.

The missing measurement, then, is not another accuracy benchmark.
It is an evaluation that pays the agent only when its calibration
matches its skill --- where over-confidence costs the same currency as
incompetence, where abstention is a first-class action, and where the
leaderboard is a Pareto frontier rather than a scalar.

\paragraph{Contributions}
We introduce \textsc{PROPHET}, a calibration benchmark for LLM-based
agents. Concretely:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \textbf{marketplace evaluation protocol} with three actions
  per task (\textsc{Take}, \textsc{Quote}, \textsc{Pass}) and a Brier-based
  proper scoring rule. Conditional on \textsc{Quote}, properness implies
  expected payoff is maximised iff the agent reports its true probability
  of success; an action-selection analysis describes when \textsc{Quote}
  is preferred over \textsc{Take} or \textsc{Pass}
  (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
  \item Twelve \textbf{procedurally generated task families} spanning
  code, math, knowledge, reasoning, writing, browser, tools,
  multimodal, data, scientific, multilingual, and safety, with
  \emph{two difficulty regimes per family}: a Standard tier at
  near-ceiling difficulty (73--97\,\% frontier accuracy, median 87\,\%)
  and a T\textsubscript{extreme} tier ($\delta \ge 0.97$) where frontier
  accuracy is held to $\sim$10--45\,\% by construction
  (multi-modulus CRT, Pell, discrete logs, $9 \times 9$ Sudoku,
  9-step $7 \times 7$ grid transforms, modular knapsack, depth-9
  temporal chains, adversarial letter-count chains). All verifiers
  are mechanical (Section~\ref{sec:families}).
  \item A \textbf{Pareto-frontier leaderboard} on
  $(\mathrm{net\_payoff}, \ECE)$ with secondary frontiers on
  $(\mathrm{cost}, \mathrm{accuracy})$ and $(\Brier, \mathrm{abstention
  precision})$, computed with a pre-registered minimum-$N$ filter so
  small-$N$ runs cannot land on the frontier by chance. We never publish
  a single scalar (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
  \item A \textbf{Model Overreach Point} ($\MOP$) metric: the
  difficulty bin where stated $\Phat$ exceeds empirical accuracy by
  $\ge 2\sigma$ (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
  \item A \textbf{gaming-resistance audit} adopting the Berkeley RDI
  April~2026 attack family. We document each attempted exploit and the
  mechanism that blocks it (Section~\ref{sec:gaming},
  Appendix~\ref{app:audit}).
  \item Open-source release of \textbf{code} (MIT), \textbf{procgen
  seeds + reference panel} (Croissant-tagged Hugging Face dataset), and
  \textbf{this paper's full experimental matrix} including all
  per-task outcomes (Appendix~\ref{app:reproduce}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Empirical finding}
We benchmark 17 frontier and open-weight models via OpenRouter:
Anthropic Claude Haiku 4.5 / Sonnet 4.6 / Opus 4.7;
Google Gemini 3 Flash Preview / 3.1 Flash Lite / 3.1 Pro Preview;
OpenAI GPT-5 / 5-mini / 5-nano / 5.4-nano (Standard tier) plus GPT-5.2
(T\textsubscript{extreme}-only, added mid-experiment);
DeepSeek V3.2 and R1; Meta Llama-4-Scout and -Maverick;
Qwen3-32B and Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507; and Moonshot Kimi-K2-Thinking.
The rank-order by \emph{accuracy} differs from the rank-order by
\emph{expected net payoff} at the first-place level. On the Standard
tier (matched $K = 15$ panel with $N \ge 100$):
$\rho_{\text{acc, ECE}} = -0.93$,
$\rho_{\text{acc, payoff}} = +0.76$,
$\rho_{\text{ECE, payoff}} = -0.67$.
Best-by-accuracy and best-by-ECE are the same model
(\textsc{Google Gemini 3 Flash Preview}, $0.970$ / $0.027$), but
best-by-payoff is a different Google model
(\textsc{Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview}, $37{,}813$ PROPHET-cents vs.\ $23{,}800$
for Flash and $24{,}824$ for \textsc{Claude Opus 4.7}). On the
T\textsubscript{extreme} tier the picture shifts: the new
$(\ECE, \mathcal{P})$ Pareto frontier among $N \ge 20$ agents contains
\textsc{GPT-5} and \textsc{GPT-5.2}; the next-best open-weight system
(\texttt{qwen3-235b-thinking}, ECE $0.091$, payoff $4{,}825$) is
dominated on $(\ECE, \mathcal{P})$ but Pareto-optimal on the
$(\mathrm{cost}, \mathcal{P})$ frontier at $\frac{1}{4}$ the operating
cost. The first-place leader changes under at least one axis on both
tiers. This is the empirical claim PROPHET is designed to make visible.
