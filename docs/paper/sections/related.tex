\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Saturation of static LLM benchmarks.}
By May 2026, the headline static-resettable benchmarks are at or past
saturation. MMLU and MMLU-Pro sit above $88\,\%$; GPQA-Diamond above
$94\,\%$; AIME 2025/26 and MATH-500 above $91\,\%$; SWE-bench Verified
at $\approx 94\,\%$ for state-of-the-art 2026 systems~\cite{swebench};
OSWorld-Verified has been crossed by recent multimodal-agent
systems~\cite{osworld}. HLE rose from $8\,\%$ (Jan 2025, o1) to
$\approx 65\,\%$ (May 2026)~\cite{hle}; ARC-AGI-2 from $53\,\%$ (Dec
2025) to $85\,\%$ (May 2026)~\cite{arcagi2}. The capabilities that did
\emph{not} saturate are all about calibrated, persistent agency:
ARC-AGI-3 $<$$1\,\%$~\cite{arcagi3}, SWE-EVO $\approx 25\,\%$,
MemoryAgentBench selective-forgetting $<$$30\,\%$~\cite{memoryagentbench}.

\paragraph{Verbal calibration of LLMs.} Kadavath
et~al.~\cite{kadavath2022} showed LLMs have non-trivial verbal
calibration on factual QA; Tian et~al.\ (2023) benchmarked verbalised
probabilities. Recent reasoning-faithfulness work
\cite{anthropic_faithfulness} found that models verbalise hints in only
25--39\,\% of cases and that \emph{unfaithful} chains-of-thought are
systematically \emph{longer} than faithful ones --- a finding directly
relevant to PROPHET's calibration emphasis.

\paragraph{Reliability beyond pass@1.} \cite{beyond_pass1} formalised
the Reliability Decay Curve, Variance Amplification Factor, and Model
Overreach Point. \cite{long_horizon_mirage} found recovery is the
dominant production failure mode. PROPHET operationalises these as
publishable scores.

\paragraph{Proper scoring rules in machine-learning evaluation.}
Brier (1950) introduced the canonical quadratic scoring rule for
probability forecasts; Gneiting and Raftery~\cite{gneiting2007} survey
the theory and practical alternatives. Guo et~al.~\cite{guo2017}
established ECE and temperature scaling as the modern calibration
benchmarks for deep classifiers. We adopt Brier as the calibration term
because it is bounded, smooth, easy to communicate, and admits closed-
form expected payoff at known $P[y=1\!\mid\!t]$.

\paragraph{Agent benchmarks and gaming.} GAIA~\cite{gaia} established
multi-step tool-use as a measurable surface; AgentBench, MMAU, OSWorld
~\cite{osworld} followed. The Berkeley RDI April~2026
audit~\cite{berkeley_rdi_audit} showed trivial exploits gave 100\,\% on
eight major agent benchmarks. We adopt their attack taxonomy as a pre-
publication red-team checklist (Section~\ref{sec:gaming}).

\paragraph{Multiple-comparison correction in benchmark papers.}
Demsar (2006) argued for non-parametric paired tests when comparing
multiple classifiers; Dem\v{s}ar's argument applies a fortiori at our
scale (twelve task families, $\sim$15 agents, three primary metrics).
We default to Holm--Bonferroni for family-wise error rate; results are
also reported under Benjamini--Hochberg FDR.

\paragraph{Concurrent benchmarks.} \cite{memoryagentbench} measures
selective forgetting; \cite{beyond_pass1} measures reliability decay;
PROPHET is complementary: it measures calibrated commitment under a
proper scoring rule across a heterogeneous task suite. None of the
existing benchmarks publishes a Pareto frontier on
$(\text{net payoff}, \mathrm{ECE})$.

\paragraph{Calibration markets and concurrent work.} A burst of
calibration-with-skin-in-the-game benchmarks appeared in 2025--2026.
\textbf{Prophet Arena}~\cite{prophet_arena} (UChicago, October 2025)
evaluates frontier LLMs on live Kalshi prediction-market events with
Brier, ECE, and a market-return metric; it shares our overall framing
but operates on \emph{live external events} rather than procedural
tasks with mechanical verifiers, has no abstention action, and admits
no Pareto comparison. \textbf{KalshiBench}, \textbf{PolyBench}, and
\textbf{ForecastBench}~\cite{forecastbench} cover similar territory and
inherit the same external-event dependency. The theoretically closest
sibling is \textbf{BAS}~\cite{bas_oxford} (Oxford, April 2026), which
defines an asymmetric-overconfidence proper-scoring rule with an
abstention option; PROPHET differs in (i) using a bounded
\emph{Brier-based} payoff $[-3\kappa, +\kappa]$ rather than an
unbounded log-style penalty, (ii) exposing an explicit
TAKE/QUOTE/PASS action menu rather than a single probability emission,
(iii) sourcing tasks from a \emph{procedurally generated} 12-family
capability surface with mechanical verifiers, and (iv) reporting a
two-dimensional Pareto frontier rather than a scalar leaderboard.
\textbf{AbstentionBench}~\cite{abstentionbench} and
\textbf{Going-All-In}~\cite{going_all_in} jointly motivate the bundle:
the former shows that current systems do not abstain even when ECE
indicates they should, the latter that prompted-confidence elicitation
diverges sharply from behavioural commitment.
\textbf{CRS}~\cite{crs_composite} explicitly proposes a single
composite reliability score; we deliberately avoid this in favour of
the Pareto view, on the grounds that distinct downstream users have
different cost-of-error vs cost-of-pass trade-offs that cannot be
captured by a single scalar.

\paragraph{Hard procedural task families.} Several recent
proc-generated benchmarks supply the kind of difficulty floor PROPHET's
T\textsubscript{extreme} tier targets. \textbf{Enigmata}~\cite{enigmata}
provides 36 puzzle generators with Python verifiers; the Sequential
Hard tier holds o4-mini at 34\,\% and o3-mini-high at 29.6\,\%.
\textbf{ZebraLogic}~\cite{zebralogic} documents a ``curse of
complexity'' where Claude~3.5 drops to 12.4\,\% on the hard
($\geq$6$\times$6 with red herrings) tier.
\textbf{MultiZebraLogic}~\cite{multizebralogic} shows that 5
inconsistent-source red herrings drop o3-mini by 15$\pm$7\,pp on
4$\times$5 puzzles. \textbf{NPPC}~\cite{nppc} ships a \texttt{npgym}
generator for 25 NP-complete problems; all frontier models score under
20\,\% past instance-complexity 4--5.
\textbf{OJBench}~\cite{ojbench}, \textbf{LiveCodeBench-Pro}~\cite{lcbpro},
and \textbf{AetherCode}~\cite{aethercode} sit at 0--10\,\% pass@1 on
their hard tiers as of May 2026. \textbf{NoLiMa}~\cite{nolima} shows
GPT-4o dropping from 99.3\,\% to 69.7\,\% on minimally-lexical-overlap
needle-in-haystack passages. PROPHET's T\textsubscript{extreme} tier
draws on the same recipe (multi-modulus CRT, discrete log,
6$\times$6 zebra, cryptarithmetic, Latin-square completion, temporal
chains, lattice-path counting) so accuracy spread is preserved at
$\sim$10--20\,\% even after the standard-difficulty tier saturates.

\paragraph{Name disambiguation.} A 2025 benchmark named
\emph{Prophet Arena}~\cite{prophet_arena} evaluates LLM forecasting on
Kalshi prediction-market events. \emph{PROPHET} (this paper) is a
distinct, procedurally-generated benchmark spanning 12 capability
families with mechanical verifiers and an explicit
TAKE/QUOTE/PASS action menu. The shared word reflects the shared
proper-scoring-rule lineage; the design choices are
non-overlapping (Section~\ref{sec:design}).
