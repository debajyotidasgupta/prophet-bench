\begin{abstract}
Frontier large-language-model agents now solve nearly every static-resettable
benchmark we have. We argue the most-deployed AI capability has become the
\emph{least-calibrated}: models systematically over-quote their probability of
success and commit to tasks they should decline, with measurable production
cost. We introduce \textbf{PROPHET}, a benchmark that evaluates LLM agents
inside a marketplace. For each task an agent must commit (\textsc{Take}),
declare its probability of success (\textsc{Quote}, scored on a Brier-based
proper scoring rule) or abstain (\textsc{Pass}). PROPHET ships 12 task
families spanning code, math, knowledge, reasoning, writing, browsing,
tool-use, multimodal, data-analysis, scientific, multilingual, and safety
domains, all procedurally generated to resist memorisation and Berkeley-style
benchmark exploits. We report the headline result as a Pareto frontier of
expected payoff against expected calibration error (ECE), abstention
precision, and the Model Overreach Point. Across \TODO{N} frontier and

\end{abstract}
