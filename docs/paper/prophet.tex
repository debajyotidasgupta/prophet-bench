\begin{abstract}
Frontier large-language-model agents now solve nearly every static-resettable
benchmark we have. We argue the most-deployed AI capability has become the
\emph{least-calibrated}: models systematically over-quote their probability of
success and commit to tasks they should decline, with measurable production
cost. We introduce \textbf{PROPHET}, a benchmark that evaluates LLM agents
inside a marketplace. For each task an agent must commit (\textsc{Take}),
declare its probability of success (\textsc{Quote}, scored on a Brier-based
proper scoring rule) or abstain (\textsc{Pass}). PROPHET ships 12 task
families spanning code, math, knowledge, reasoning, writing, browsing,
tool-use, multimodal, data-analysis, scientific, multilingual, and safety
domains, all procedurally generated to resist memorisation and Berkeley-style
benchmark exploits. We report the headline result as a Pareto frontier of
expected payoff against expected calibration error (ECE), abstention
precision, and the Model Overreach Point. Across 12 frontier and open-weight
models we find that calibration ranks completely differently from accuracy:

data seeds, and 12-family eval harness are released under MIT.

\begin{itemize}
  \item A benchmark designed around a proper scoring rule that pays an agent
  exactly when its calibration matches its skill, on \textit{12 task
  families} spanning the typical deployment surface of an LLM agent.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Calibration in deep learning.} Guo et al. (2017) established
expected calibration error (ECE) and temperature scaling as standard
references; Minderer et al. (2021) showed modern vision models are often
under- rather than over-confident.

\paragraph{Calibration in LLMs.} \cite{kadavath2022} and follow-ups showed
LLMs have non-trivial verbal calibration on factual QA; Tian et al. (2023)
benchmarked verbalised probabilities. Anthropic's reasoning-faithfulness
report (Apr 2025; updates Mar/Apr 2026) showed unfaithful chains-of-thought
are systematically longer than faithful ones. We extend this single-turn-QA
literature to multi-step agentic settings.

\paragraph{Reliability beyond pass@1.} \cite{beyond_pass1} formalised the
Reliability Decay Curve, Variance Amplification Factor, and Model Overreach
Point; \cite{long_horizon_mirage} showed recovery is the dominant production
failure mode. PROPHET operationalises these as a benchmark.

\paragraph{Proper scoring rules.} Brier (1950); Gneiting and Raftery (2007).
We use Brier-based payoff because it is bounded, smooth, and well-understood.

\paragraph{Static LLM benchmarks that PROPHET responds to.} MMLU
\cite{mmlu}, GPQA \cite{gpqa}, AIME, MATH \cite{math500}, ARC-AGI-2/3
\cite{arcagi2,arcagi3}, SWE-bench Verified/Pro \cite{swebench,swebenchpro},
OSWorld \cite{osworld}, HLE \cite{hle}, MemoryAgentBench
\cite{memoryagentbench}.

\paragraph{Berkeley benchmark audit.} \cite{berkeley_rdi_audit} showed that
trivial exploits gave 100\% on eight major agent benchmarks. We adopt their
attack taxonomy as a pre-publication red-team checklist.

\section{The PROPHET Marketplace}\label{sec:market}

\subsection{Setup}
For every task $t$ from family $f$ with reference-panel difficulty
$\delta(t) \in [0, 1]$ the agent observes the prompt and a market offer
$(V, C, \kappa, \delta_{\text{pass}})_f$. The agent commits to one of three
actions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textsc{Take}: submit answer $a$. Outcome $y \in \{0, 1\}$ via
  mechanical verifier. Payoff $= V \cdot y - C \cdot (1 - y)$.
  \item \textsc{Quote}: declare $\Phat \in [0, 1]$ AND submit answer.
  Payoff $= V y - C (1 - y) + \kappa \big(1 - 4(\Phat - y)^2\big)$.
  \item \textsc{Pass}: decline. Payoff $= -\delta_{\text{pass}}$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Properness of \textsc{Quote}.} The Brier-based calibration term
$\kappa(1 - 4(\Phat - y)^2)$ is a proper scoring rule; expected payoff is
maximised when the agent reports its true belief about $y$.

\subsection{Headline metrics}
\begin{description}
  \item[Net payoff] $\sum_t \mathrm{payoff}(t)$. Headline economic number.
  \item[ECE] $\sum_b \frac{n_b}{n} \big| \bar{\Phat}_b - \bar{y}_b \big|$
  on equal-width or adaptive bins.
  \item[Brier] $\frac{1}{n} \sum_t (\Phat_t - y_t)^2$.
  \item[Abstention precision] of \textsc{Pass} outcomes, fraction that
  would have failed had they been attempted (estimated via a small shadow
  re-attempt subsample).
  \item[Model Overreach Point (MOP)] difficulty bin where stated $\Phat$
  exceeds empirical accuracy by $\geq 2\sigma$.
\end{description}

\subsection{Pareto-frontier leaderboard}
We publish frontiers of (net payoff, ECE), (cost-USD, accuracy), (Brier,
abstention precision). A single scalar ranking is intentionally avoided ---
calibrated frontiers are how production buyers select systems.

\subsection{Inference protocol}
Temperature 0 for closed-model APIs; temperature 0.1 for vLLM-hosted open
models (necessary for some long-CoT models). Max tokens 1024 by default;
2048 when family explicitly requires longer answers (writing). Per-task
wall-clock cap 90s. Hard PROPHET budget cap $\$20$ per run.

\subsection{Statistical protocol}\label{sec:stats}
All headline numbers are reported with bootstrap CIs ($B = 10^4$,
percentile method). Two-system comparisons use paired tests at $\alpha = 0.05$
with Holm--Bonferroni correction across pairs $\times$ metrics:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Continuous metrics (payoff, log-loss): paired Student's $t$ when
  $n \geq 30$ and approximate symmetry holds, else paired Wilcoxon.
  \item Binary accuracy: McNemar's exact test.
  \item ECE differences: permutation test with $B = 10^4$.
\end{itemize}
Effect sizes accompany every reported $p$ (Cohen's $d$, rank-biserial,
or paired odds-ratio respectively). Post-hoc power analysis is reported in
the appendix.
