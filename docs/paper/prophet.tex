\begin{abstract}
Frontier large-language-model agents now solve nearly every
static-resettable benchmark we have, yet a 37\,\% gap between lab
accuracy and production reliability persists. The capability we are
mis-measuring is \emph{calibrated agency} --- knowing when to commit,
declining tasks one should not take, and pricing one's own confidence
with skin in the game. We introduce \textbf{PROPHET},\footnote{Distinct from
\emph{Prophet Arena}~\cite{prophet_arena} (UChicago, October 2025), which
benchmarks LLM forecasting on live Kalshi markets with natural events.
PROPHET is a procedurally-generated, mechanically-verified, two-tier
calibration benchmark with a Brier-scaled marketplace; the two suites
are complementary, not overlapping.} a benchmark that evaluates
LLM-based agents inside a marketplace. For each task, the
agent must commit (\textsc{Take}), declare a probability of success
(\textsc{Quote}, scored on a Brier-based proper scoring rule conditional
on the Quote action) or abstain (\textsc{Pass}). PROPHET ships 12
procedurally-generated task families spanning code, math, knowledge,
reasoning, writing, browser, tool-use, multimodal, data-analysis,
scientific, multilingual, and safety domains, evaluated at \emph{two
difficulty regimes}: a \textbf{Standard tier} where frontier accuracy is
near-ceiling (90--97\,\%) yet ECE still varies $\sim$$7\times$ across
systems, and a \textbf{T\textsubscript{extreme} tier} ($\delta \ge 0.97$)
with multi-modulus CRT, Pell equations, discrete logarithms, depth-9
temporal chains, $9\times 9$ Sudoku, modular knapsack, lattice-path
counting, and Einstein/zebra puzzles, where frontier accuracy drops to
$\sim$10--40\,\% by design. All verifiers are mechanical. We benchmark
17 frontier and open-weight systems via OpenRouter (Anthropic
\texttt{claude-haiku-4.5}/\texttt{sonnet-4.6}/\texttt{opus-4.7}; Google
\texttt{gemini-3-flash-preview}/\texttt{3.1-flash-lite}/\texttt{3.1-pro-preview};
OpenAI \texttt{gpt-5}/\texttt{5-mini}/\texttt{5-nano}/\texttt{5.2}/\texttt{5.4-nano};
DeepSeek \texttt{v3.2}/\texttt{r1}; Meta \texttt{llama-4-scout}/\texttt{maverick};
Qwen \texttt{qwen3-32b}/\texttt{235b-thinking}; Moonshot
\texttt{kimi-k2-thinking}). \texttt{gpt-5.2} was added on
T\textsubscript{extreme} only owing to its mid-experiment release.
Headline metrics use a pre-registered minimum-$N$ filter ($N \ge 100$
Standard, $N \ge 20$ T\textsubscript{extreme}) so small-$N$ outliers do
not dominate the leaderboard. The headline finding from the
Standard tier: \textbf{the first-place leader differs between accuracy
and net payoff.} Best by accuracy and best by ECE: Google Gemini 3
Flash Preview ($0.970$, ECE $0.027$). Best by net payoff: Google Gemini
3.1 Pro Preview ($37{,}813$ PROPHET-cents, vs.\ $23{,}800$ for the Flash
sibling and $24{,}824$ for Claude Opus 4.7) --- the same vendor at two
different price-points, separated by commitment policy not by accuracy.
On the matched $K = 15$ panel Spearman $\rho_{\text{acc, ECE}} = -0.93$,
$\rho_{\text{acc, payoff}} = +0.76$, $\rho_{\text{ECE, payoff}} = -0.67$.
On T\textsubscript{extreme} the picture shifts further: the new
$(\ECE, \mathcal{P})$ Pareto frontier is \texttt{gpt-5} (ECE $0.019$,
payoff $5{,}274$) and \texttt{gpt-5.2} (ECE $0.025$, payoff $10{,}500$);
median accuracy collapses from $87\,\%$ to $60\,\%$ ($-27$\,pp) and
median ECE worsens $2.12\times$. All code, procgen seeds, agent
adapters, and per-family eval harness are released under MIT and on
Hugging Face for re-execution at any future model checkpoint.
\end{abstract}
