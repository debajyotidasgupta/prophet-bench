\begin{abstract}
Frontier large-language-model agents now solve nearly every static-resettable
benchmark we have, yet a 37\,\% gap between lab accuracy and production
reliability persists. The capability we are mis-measuring is
\emph{calibrated agency} --- knowing when to commit, declining tasks one
should not take, and pricing one's own confidence with skin in the game.
We introduce \textbf{PROPHET}, a benchmark that evaluates LLM-based
agents inside a marketplace. For each task, the agent must commit
(\textsc{Take}), declare a probability of success (\textsc{Quote},
scored on a Brier-based proper scoring rule) or abstain (\textsc{Pass}).
PROPHET ships 12 procedurally-generated task families spanning code,
math, knowledge, reasoning, writing, browser, tool-use, multimodal,
data-analysis, scientific, multilingual, and safety domains. All
verifiers are mechanical. We benchmark 13 frontier and open-weight
systems via OpenRouter (Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, DeepSeek, Meta,
Qwen, Moonshot). The headline finding: \textbf{the first-place leader
changes under every headline axis.} Best by accuracy: Google Gemini 3
Flash Preview (97.0\,\%). Best by ECE: Gemini 3 Flash Preview (0.027).
Best by net payoff: Anthropic Claude Opus 4.7 (23,686 PROPHET-cents
vs.\ 22,908 for Gemini). Both are Pareto-optimal on $(\ECE, \mathcal{P})$;
neither single scalar would have selected the right model for a
risk-aware buyer. Spearman rank correlations between the three axes
range $0.73$--$0.89$, showing the orderings are not interchangeable
even at $n = 12$ frontier systems. All code, procgen seeds, agent
adapters, and 240-task-per-family eval harness are released under MIT
and on Hugging Face for re-execution at any future model checkpoint.
\end{abstract}
